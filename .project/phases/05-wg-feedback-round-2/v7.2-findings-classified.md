## 1a — Standards decisions (2)

### NC2 — An empty resolution: does the opportunity survive?

**The standard** (§5.16.2.2.6, PDF 11587/11589): *"Execution fails if at
least one of the conditions below is true at PRTA: — APDA is determined
to be 0."* And §5.16.2.2.5 (PDF 11563): *"If execution fails, steps a-c
above are repeated for next events in QE."* NOTE 3 (PDF 11618): *"The
counter E.c has not been incremented due to the failure, consequently if
E.c = 0 the event can still be executed in the future even if the value
of `@executeOnce` is `"true"`."*

**We say** (§4.6.8, from R30 and R20.1): an empty resolution is a
completed, successful resolution. The Player continues with the primary
content, does not fall through to a fallback window, and the opportunity
is spent.

**Scope — smaller than it first looked.** The *visible behaviour* does
not diverge: PDF 11616 says *"A failed execution results in smooth
continued playback of the main media presentation"*, which is what we
want. And DASH's "failure" means "produced no alternative content this
time", not "error". What actually diverges is two consequences:

1. the base model **tries the next event in the queue**; we do not;
2. the base model **does not consume the opportunity** — it can execute
   later, even under `@executeOnce="true"`; we treat it as spent.

**Why it cannot resolve itself.** Aligning costs the fallback semantics
R20.1 was written for, and makes an unfilled slot retryable — which is
revenue-positive but adds Player state and a second resolution request
against the same opportunity. Diverging costs conformance with a clause
the base model spells out, and has to be declared as a deliberate
extension rather than inherited.

**To close it**: his decision. Then a `context/` edit and a major build.

### M4 — The profile procedure strips our normative ClickThrough carrier

**The standard** (§8.1, profile-specific MPD, step 4): *"All elements or
attributes that are either (i) in this document and explicitly excluded
by ProfA, or (ii) **in an extension namespace and not explicitly
included by ProfA**, are removed from the profile-specific MPD."*

**We say** (R28, §5.6): the ClickThrough carrier is *normative and
interoperable* — the reason it exists rather than riding the best-effort
metadata carrier of R23 is that the click must work on every conformant
Player.

**The conflict.** A `ListMPD` whose `@profiles` names only
`urn:mpeg:dash:profile:list:2024` loses `<svta:Click>` for conformance
purposes, and a Player bound to that profile alone is entitled to drop
it. We mint a profile URI for the non-linear document only, so the
linear path has nothing to declare that would include our namespace.
**R28's cross-Player guarantee does not hold on the linear path** — the
one property that distinguishes it from R23.

**Why it cannot resolve itself.** Minting a profile URI for the linear
document means a second URI to define, publish and get implementers to
declare. Not minting it means R28 is best-effort on the linear path,
which contradicts what R28 says about itself. A third option — moving
the carrier out of the extension namespace — is not ours to take.

**To close it**: his decision. Then `context/` work in either direction.

# Movements

A classification that cannot move is one that stopped looking. Each
movement records where the item came from, where it went, and the
evidence that moved it — the destination alone is useless to a reader
three months from now.

## M2 — from 2 to 1b

**Moved by**: §8.14, PDF 15350. Declaring `MPD@type="list"` is not a
free attribute value: it constitutes the ISO-BMFF List profile, whose
rule 2 requires `urn:mpeg:dash:profile:list:2024` in `MPD@profiles`.
The suggested "no trade-off" fix therefore binds the non-linear
resolution document into the linear profile. It travels with M4.

## M4 — from 1a to a wording fix, already applied

**Moved by**: §8.1 NOTE 1, PDF 13335 — *"profiles merely specify
restrictions on MPD and Segments rather than DASH Client behaviour"* —

### R14 is neither a decision nor a generation defect

Both halves are in `context/`, and they are not a design conflict.
R14's body anchors itself explicitly: a non-linear slot may carry more
than one form *"played in **sequence**, exactly as a linear ad break
plays its candidate ads one after another (R7)"*, with an example of
three overlays. The unit that sequences is therefore the **candidate**,
as R7 already says.

What R14 and R14.1 then do is **call that unit a form**, while R5.1
defines a form as one component of a presentation *option*, and R5.2
and R5.6 make options alternatives — the Player renders the first one
it can satisfy. One word, two objects.

The correct wording is recoverable from R14's own body, so nothing is
lost either way and nobody has to choose. It is a terminology
correction across R14, R14.1 and R14.2, not a product decision.

**And the generator did not misread anything.** It reproduced the
collision `context/` handed it. What went wrong is one level up: the
validator recorded a `context/`-level contradiction as *partial
coverage*, and this classification agreed with it. Two instruments
agreeing is not corroboration when one of them is reading the other's
summary.

### R21 is a decision, and its root is in `context/`

R12 gives Overlay two named placements and Squeezeback two, and gives
**Pause-ad one token** — `pause`, described as *"fullscreen or partial
(see R21)"*. R21 then states that which surface applies *"is a property
of the pause-ad form / layout the Player selects (R5)"*.

R5 selects among options identified by layout tokens, and the pause
family has one. A Publisher's `@allowedLayouts` cannot admit fullscreen
while excluding partial, and an APS cannot offer the two as distinct
options. **R21 delegates a choice to a vocabulary that does not carry
it**, and either splitting the token — as Overlay and Squeezeback
already are — or withdrawing R21's delegation costs something.

## Method — two different ways a classification goes wrong

**M2 and M4 moved because new evidence arrived**: a clause nobody had
read yet. Nothing could have prevented those; finding them is the work.

**The ten moved because the existing evidence was read badly.** The
cell was there the whole time. That is the cheap failure to prevent,
and it is the one that feels safest from the inside, because the
reader is certain they have read.

**And this batch was caught by a warning, not by a review.** Before
these ten were looked at again, the coordinating agent said that
*"partial coverage sounds mechanical and is not"*. That sentence is
what caused the cell to be read. Recording it as a review would tell
the next reader that reviewing finds this class of error, and reviewing
is exactly what had already passed over it.
